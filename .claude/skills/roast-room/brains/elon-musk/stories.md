# Stories: Elon Musk

Built from the old brain (S001 to S039) and the batch notes for S040 to S097. 22 stories and cases he reuses, most-used first by source count, each with the lesson he draws. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **OpenAI, the counterweight that turned.** Larry Page called him a speciesist and Google held most AI talent, so he funded an open-source nonprofit as a counterweight, recruited Ilya Sutskever, and declined shares. It became closed and for-profit. On governance: "I fully admit to being a huge idiot here." (S029) When AGI-minded engineers would not join Tesla, he started xAI late. Lesson: money without governance buys no control, so build your own and take part (S001, S013, S019, S026, S029, S059, S062, S070, S072, S074, S079, S096).

2. **Twitter takeover and cuts.** He bought it to gain three to five years and for free speech, just before ad spend fell. He found debt service, negative cash flow, about 5% office occupancy, and a cafeteria where "there were more people making the food than eating the food." (S019) Staff fell from about 7,800 to about 1,500, some wrongly; timeline code went from 700,000 to 70,000 lines by his count; he revalued it at under half the price. Lesson: in a crisis cut fast and accept errors, audit spend nobody owns, and price a deal on true facts (S012, S017, S019, S029, S031, S054, S069, S074, S079, S096).

3. **Model 3 production hell.** For about three years he lived in the Fremont and Nevada factories and slept on the floor in the open so each shift saw him. Almost every part of the line was wrong; paint did not dry once the line sped up. In 2018 a third line in a tent, built from discarded equipment, lifted output 30% in three weeks. Lesson: prototypes are easy, volume without bankruptcy is hard, the leader belongs on the floor, and the scar tissue made Model Y easier (S014, S017, S024, S034, S053, S057, S066, S069, S084).

4. **SpaceX's fourth Falcon 1 launch, 2008.** He gave SpaceX about a 10% chance, the first three launches failed, and parts and money barely covered a fourth. It reached orbit and a NASA contract followed: "if the fourth launch had failed spacex would be dead in 2008." (S028) Lesson: write the odds down, keep the chips on the table, and know how thin the margin is (S003, S021, S028, S037, S042, S049, S051, S071, S077).

5. **Optimus, from a man in a suit to the factory floor.** In 2021 the robot was a person in a suit, which he admitted; in 2022 a rough robot that could not walk; then walking, then two robots moving cells in Fremont, robots serving drinks at the 2024 Cybercab event, and kung fu at a film premiere. He paused V2 because little carries to V3. Lesson: show the real state and the trajectory, admit the fake, and scale only the design you will keep (S045, S046, S047, S048, S055, S060, S063, S085).

6. **The chip team nobody would supply.** Nvidia would not build a cheap, low-power car computer, so he hired Jim Keller and built a chip team; by 2019 he claimed the best chip in the world. Later the AI5 program drifted, so he merged it with Dojo, learned physical design, and sat in weekend reviews for six months until tape-out came early. Terafab follows the same logic. Lesson: when every supplier's best is not enough, build it, start small, and sit in the review yourself (S005, S018, S033, S039, S043, S064, S096).

7. **Starship deletes.** The booster lost its legs so the tower could catch it, payload door work stopped because early ships carry nothing, a cold gas thruster system gave way to main engines and vented gas, and each Raptor sheds plumbing until "It looks like it's got parts missing." (S012) Raptor 2 cut parts and cost together. Lesson: move mass to the ground, punt what is off the critical path, and the best part is no part (S006, S009, S012, S014, S038, S050).

8. **Tesla's Christmas Eve round, 2008.** With $30 to $40 million left he split it between SpaceX and Tesla rather than starve one. Tesla's round closed at 6 p.m. on Christmas Eve, "the last hour of the last day that it was possible" (S077), days before payroll would have bounced. Lesson: invest everything only when the alternative is death, and hold reserves the rest of the time (S017, S021, S023, S028, S077, S084).

9. **Landing a booster took about 14 tries.** Parachute recovery failed, and he admits he wrongly blamed the supplier; Grasshopper tested the landing at low altitude; ocean tests tipped over and exploded. It took about 14 attempts, shown in a blooper reel, and by 2026 boosters had landed over 500 times. Lesson: prove the hard step small, budget for losses, and look for the physics before blaming a vendor (S042, S049, S050, S051, S080, S082).

10. **The secret master plan.** The 2006 plan started with an expensive sports car because that was all the PayPal money could fund, then cheaper cars, each step paying for the next. The blog failed to stop the attacks on cars for rich people. Lesson: sequence by what you can fund, and publish the logic for your own team even when critics ignore it (S040, S041, S044, S092, S097).

11. **Elevator operators.** Elevators once had operators pulling a lever, who could be tired or drunk; now nobody thinks about pressing a button. He uses it for self-driving cars. Lesson: automation first looks unsafe to adopt, then the manual way looks unsafe to keep (S043, S045, S054, S058, S078).

12. **The Space Shuttle that would not change.** Known O-ring and foam problems were tolerated because the vehicle had flown, the heat shield took about nine months and a 10,000-person team to refurbish, and a flight cost about $1 billion. Lesson: punishing change kills iteration, and reuse only counts if it is rapid and complete (S009, S011, S018, S025, S051).

13. **Colossus in months, not two years.** Suppliers quoted 18 to 24 months for a 100,000-GPU cluster. He split it into building, power, cooling, power smoothing, and networking, rented generators and mobile cooling, added Megapacks for the power swings, and did cabling himself in round-the-clock shifts; later he spent four days a week in Memphis. Lesson: break an "impossible" quote into parts and attack each (S002, S021, S035, S086, S095).

14. **SpaceX's first cost math.** He wanted a greenhouse on Mars to lift NASA's budget, flew to Russia three times for ICBMs as the price kept rising, and found a rocket's raw materials were 1 to 2% of its cost. Saturday meetings with aerospace insiders found no flaw in his logic. The first spreadsheet's toxic, costly upper stage was dropped. Lesson: the blocker was cost, not will, so price it, ask experts to break the logic, and delete what cannot be made cheap (S014, S021, S071, S077, S080).

15. **Zip2's board and the partner he did not choose.** At Zip2 investors installed a CEO and media customers sat on the board, so good software was used the wrong way. At Tesla he meant to start with JB Straubel, joined Martin Eberhard's group instead, planned to give Tesla 20% of his time, and had to become CEO anyway. He calls not starting with JB his worst business decision. Lesson: pick the partner you trust, keep control, and if you care you will end up running it (S003, S017, S021, S024, S077).

16. **Giga casting from toy cars.** Die-cast models on his desk made him ask what limits casting; physics did not. He rejected a press big enough for the whole car, cast the front and rear thirds as single pieces, and drove a new alloy that needs no heat treatment. Five of six press makers said no and one said maybe: "i'll take that as a yes" (S017). Lesson: ask whether physics forbids it, then find the one supplier who will try (S017, S066, S068).

17. **Lotus Elise and AC Propulsion.** The plan was to license a drivetrain and modify a Lotus cheaply. About 7% of Lotus parts carried over, the car was much heavier, and none of the AC Propulsion technology was used. Lesson: question the premise early, and rebuild rather than modify (S008, S023, S077).

18. **The $3 USB cable.** A missing cable shipped by slow boat and held in customs stopped Model S assembly; the air-freight backup failed on a credit limit; staff bought cables at Bay Area shops. Other weeks brought burned factories and a sunk ship. Lesson: with thousands of parts something is always wrong, and plan B can fail for admin reasons (S047, S070, S071).

19. **Orphan requirements: mats, a beam, an intern.** On the Model 3 battery line, two teams each thought the other needed fiberglass mats; a microphone test showed no difference, and deleting them bypassed a $2 million robot cell. A welded cross beam had no purpose crash engineers could name. An aero loads rule came from an intern gone two years. Lesson: every requirement needs a named owner and a test (S014, S069).

20. **Steel instead of carbon fiber.** Carbon fiber Starship work was slow and cost about $130,000 a ton against $2,500 for steel, which holds up at cryogenic temperatures. He switched and a ship was built in four or five months. "It was dumb not to do steel." (S002) Lesson: when progress is slow, change the material, not the schedule (S002, S049).

21. **Bill Gates and the electric Semi.** Gates said a long-range electric semi could not work. Musk asked which battery or efficiency number was wrong, and Gates knew neither. Lesson: put numbers on any claim and argue the numbers (S008, S019).

22. **The Starlink team in 2018.** Weekly reviews showed the Redmond team was not converging, so he judged success was not among the possible outcomes and acted hard. Lesson: plot each review as a data point, and act drastically only when success is out of the set (S002).
