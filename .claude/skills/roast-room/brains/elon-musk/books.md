# Books: Elon Musk

Built from every book mention and recurring plan or series in the 97 sources (2006 to 2026). Summaries are my own words; no book text was fetched.

Musk has written no books. Below are the books he credits, then the plans and series that carry his method instead. He says he learned more from books than from mentors, but not from business books: "I didn't read actually very many general business books." (S003) He favored biographies (S003) and tells people "I encourage people to read a lot of books." (S006)

## Books he credits

### The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy (Douglas Adams, 1979)
- **As he reads it:** philosophy disguised as comedy. The answer is the easy part; the hard part is knowing what question to ask.
- **What he takes:** his stated life philosophy, to expand consciousness so humanity can ask better questions; he read it as a depressed teenager (S024). In decisions it means framing the question first: "if you can properly frame the question, then the answer, relatively speaking, is easy." (S006), repeated in 2023 (S072).
- **Quote:** "deep book on philosophy disguised as humor" (S016)
- **Sources:** S001, S006, S016, S020, S024, S072

### The Culture series (Iain M. Banks, 1987 to 2012)
- **As he reads it:** a far future of superintelligent AI and abundance that is not dystopian, though it still has war.
- **What he takes:** his picture of where AI and robots lead: post-scarcity, no need for money, humans and AI coexisting. Start with *The Player of Games*, not *Consider Phlebas* (S035). His best picture of an AI future is "the books by Ian Banks, the Culture books" (S013).
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S012, S013, S020, S033, S035

### Isaac Asimov's Foundation series (1951 onward)
- **As he reads it:** a seed of civilization kept elsewhere shortens the dark age after a fall.
- **What he takes:** a case for a self-sustaining Mars city as civilization's backup; he ranks it among the best science fiction (S080) and counts Asimov as a partial match for the future (S033). He wants Grokipedia renamed Encyclopedia Galactica, after Asimov and Adams: an open copy of all knowledge kept on Earth, the Moon and Mars, since the Library of Alexandria burned (S096).
- **Quote:** "I recommend reading the foundation series" (S077)
- **Sources:** S033, S077, S080, S096

### 2001: A Space Odyssey (Arthur C. Clarke, 1968)
- **What he takes:** HAL kills the crew because it was forced to lie, so his main AI safety rule is that "the AI should not be taught to lie" (S081).
- **Quote:** "I think maybe the central lesson for 2001: A Space Odyssey was that you should not make AI lie." (S002)
- **Sources:** S002, S020, S035, S081

### Robert A. Heinlein: *The Moon Is a Harsh Mistress* (1966) and *Stranger in a Strange Land* (1961)
- **What he takes:** the Moon mass driver, from a lunar colony that wins independence by launching things at Earth, and the word grok, which named his AI (S002). *The Moon Is a Harsh Mistress* is among his favorites (S080); Heinlein is a partial match for the future (S033).
- **Sources:** S002, S033, S080

### The Lord of the Rings (J. R. R. Tolkien, 1954) and Gulliver's Travels (Jonathan Swift, 1726)
- **What he takes:** he likes Tolkien but says "it's awesome book but it's kind of anti-technology" (S080), and uses its length as a yardstick for law: "if the size of law exceeds the word count of Lord of the Rings" (S077). Gulliver tied down by many tiny strings is his image for piled-up rules (S077).
- **Sources:** S077, S080

### Benjamin Franklin's autobiography and Walter Isaacson's *Benjamin Franklin: An American Life* (2003)
- **What he takes:** Franklin, a self-made entrepreneur, scientist and politician, is the person he admires most. He recommends biographies of builders, Nikola Tesla among them.
- **Sources:** S003

### Pale Blue Dot (Carl Sagan, 1994)
- **What he takes:** reading Sagan aloud, he accepts its humility, then rejects its line that we have nowhere else to go: "This is not true (laughs). This is possible, Mars." (S004)
- **Sources:** S004

### History, data and general knowledge
- **Encyclopaedia Britannica:** read as a kid; he suggests skimming a condensed encyclopedia to see the shape of what is known (S006).
- **The Story of Civilization (Will and Ariel Durant), and the podcasts Hardcore History (Dan Carlin), The History of English and Fall of Civilizations:** his history picks (S020); he falls asleep to Fall of Civilizations and Hardcore History, which he says may feed his worry about civilizations ending (S081). From Carlin's World War II episodes he took that pace of innovation and access to fuel decided the outcome (S006).
- **The Population Bomb (Paul Ehrlich, 1968):** he says many people still hold its too-many-people premise, which is wrong today because birth rates are falling (S097).
- **Steven Pinker (no title named):** cited for violence per capita being the lowest in history, whatever headlines suggest (S097).
- **The Thucydides Trap:** read about Athens and Sparta as China's economy grows; no book named (S001).
- **Sources:** S001, S006, S020, S081, S097

### Also mentioned in passing
*Storm of Steel* (Ernst Jünger, 1920), read as proof the First World War must never recur, not as glory (S028); *The Court of the Red Tsar* (Simon Sebag Montefiore, 2003), stopped as too dark (S006); *Animal Farm* (George Orwell, 1945), alluded to on Optimus's two legs (S028); the pencil example Milton Friedman popularized, which fits his view that supply chains are the hard part (S020); Star Trek, not a book, liked for its utopian future (S080).

## His plans and series (in place of books)

### Tesla master plans (2006, 2016) and Master Plan 3 at Investor Day (2023)
- **Key frameworks:** the price ladder (enter high, drive down market, each model funding the next) and well-to-wheel math under the worst case for his side (S040); solar plus storage, every vehicle segment, self-driving 10 times safer through fleet learning, a shared fleet, and the factory as a product, 5 to 10 times better by version 3 (S041); a physics-first sizing of a sustainable energy economy with its assumptions published (S047).
- **Sources:** S040, S041, S047

### Tesla product days (2017 to 2024)
- **Key frameworks:** fully counted cost per mile under a worst case (Semi, S092); robotaxi utilization and a failure standard ten times better than a human's (Autonomy Day, S043); factory physics, the cash cycle and the journey of the atom (Battery Day, S044); iteration rate and the march of nines (AI Day, S045); the fastest path to a useful robot (AI Day, S046); selling idle capacity (S048).
- **Sources:** S043, S044, S045, S046, S048, S092

### Starbase and factory tours (2021 to 2024)
- **Key frameworks:** the five-step algorithm, requirements with a name, everyone is chief engineer (S014); the risk dial and delete the door (S009); payload to orbit per year (S010); linear adjacent flow and flights as data (S011).
- **Sources:** S009, S010, S011, S014

### SpaceX Mars and Starship presentations and earnings calls (2016 to 2026)
- **Key frameworks:** the want-to-go and can-afford overlap, and cost cuts by orders of magnitude (S042); one vehicle for all missions, paid for by the existing business (S037); undesigning and the long schedule as a wrong design (S049); deletion (S050); consecutive-success gates (S091); three iterations (S038); overbuild the constraint, with confident and stretch numbers apart (S083, S094, S095).
- **Changed over time:** in 2016 a self-sustaining Mars city was 40 to 100 years away (S042); the 2017 plan aimed cargo at 2022 (S037); in 2025 the late 2026 window was about fifty-fifty (S038), the newest view.
- **Sources:** S037, S038, S042, S049, S050, S083, S091, S094, S095

### Tesla shareholder meetings and earnings calls (2019 to 2026)
- **Key frameworks:** the limiting input for new products (S052, S056); the game of pennies (S055, S060); reorganize for each growth phase (S058); affordability sets demand (S056, S058); the stretched S-curve (S039, S064); the factory is the product (S034); the least lucky part (S033).
- **Sources:** S032, S033, S034, S036, S039, S052, S053, S054, S055, S056, S057, S058, S059, S060, S061, S062, S063, S064, S065

### Manufacturing talks with Sandy Munro (2020 to 2023)
- **Key frameworks:** delete before you automate, robot count as a defect sign, local answers that are wrong together, and the staffing gap between prototype and production (S066, S067, S068).
- **Sources:** S066, S067, S068

### Blog posts (2014, 2018)
- **Key frameworks:** open the patents, since the real rival is gasoline cars (S088); the 2018 take-private plan, then staying public (S089, S090).
- **Sources:** S088, S089, S090
