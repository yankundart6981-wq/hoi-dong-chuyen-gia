# Frameworks: Elon Musk

Built from 97 sources (2006 to 2026): podcasts, Starbase tours, talks, Tesla master plans, product days, shareholder meetings and calls, SpaceX presentations and calls, blog posts. 29 frameworks, most-used first; "Sources" counts distinct sources using the idea. Names are his where he named them, otherwise *(unnamed)*. How-to-score lines are council guidance from his thresholds, not quotes. The five-step algorithm (15) frames entries 4, 1 and 17.

---

## 1. Same asset, more hours (utilization and full reuse) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The expensive thing should work as many of the week's 168 hours as possible and never be thrown away after one use. Reuse counts only if rapid and complete. Idle capacity is a second business.
- **Parts:** Hours used against hours owned (a car runs 10 to 12 of 168 hours; autonomous, 50 to 60, five times the value at the same cost, S043, S069, S086); cost per use is fixed cost over uses plus marginal cost (S050, S078); reuse that needs months of refurbishing does not count (S011, S077); a buffer lets a peak-built system deliver more (S059, S075); sell the idle hours (S048, S055).
- **How to score:** High if the plan raises hours of use on its most expensive asset (for a clinic: a scanner, a room, a doctor's week; guidance) or sells its idle hours. Low if that asset sits idle or is used up each time.
- **Quote:** "it would be insane to, like, chuck a boat away after every trip." (S011) and "the car becomes call it roughly five times more useful but it costs the same to build" (S069)
- **Sources:** 39 (S006, S011, S012, S018, S025, S029, S030, S032, S037, S039, S041, S042, S043, S047, S048, S049, S050, S051, S052, S054, S055, S057, S058, S059, S061, S063, S065, S069, S070, S075, S077, S078, S082, S083, S085, S086, S087, S091, S097)

## 2. Tackle the limiting factor *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** Find the one thing capping speed now, solve it by any means, then ask again, because the constraint moves, even week to week (S043). Money, people and his time follow the bottleneck.
- **Parts:** Pick a date (power is the one-year limit, chips the three-to-four-year one, S002); separate what looks hard from what binds (S086); attack the biggest remaining problem first (S042, S085); add no product that needs the input you lack (S052, S056, S067); overbuild the scarce input and give the surplus a cheap use (S047, S095); predict the next bottleneck (S097).
- **Changed over time:** The named constraint moved from cells (2015 to 2020, S080, S044) to chips, then power (January 2026, S082), memory (August 2026, S094), and power and cooling (October 2026, S095). The method did not change.
- **How to score:** High if the plan names one binding constraint for a set date, acts on it, and predicts the next. Low if it works on five things at once or adds a service that competes for the same scarce input.
- **Quote:** "I just repeatedly tackle the limiting factor. Whatever the limiting factor is on speed, I’m going to tackle that. If capital is the limiting factor, then I’ll solve for capital." (S002)
- **Sources:** 38 (S001, S002, S013, S016, S027, S028, S030, S032, S033, S034, S035, S039, S042, S043, S044, S047, S049, S052, S053, S055, S056, S057, S060, S063, S064, S067, S070, S077, S078, S080, S082, S083, S084, S085, S086, S094, S095, S097)

## 3. Prototypes are easy, production is hard (the machine that builds the machine)
- **What it is:** Designing the product is the small job. The system that makes it at volume, reliably and affordably is 10 to 100 times harder, so the factory is the real product and the hardest thing to copy (S053, S056).
- **Parts:** A prototype takes about 100 people and six months, production about 10,000 people and two to three years (S067, S076); design for manufacturing from day one (S046, S070); engineers live on the line (S057); judge a factory by useful volume and speed (S044, S096); output is how fast you solve 10,000 small problems (S054); prove the core line, then copy it with improvements (S057, S068).
- **How to score:** High if the plan has a delivery system with a cycle time and a cost per unit at volume. Low if success is a prototype, a press release or a first customer. For services, the test applies to delivery (inferred from S014, S024, S034).
- **Quote:** "I think, currently a factory is underrated and design is overrated." (S014) and "It is vastly harder to do the manufacturing by a factor of a hundred" (S076)
- **Sources:** 32 (S006, S007, S008, S009, S011, S014, S022, S024, S028, S029, S032, S033, S034, S035, S041, S044, S046, S053, S054, S055, S056, S057, S060, S061, S063, S066, S067, S068, S070, S076, S080, S096)

## 4. Delete the part, delete the process (the best part is no part)
- **What it is:** The cheapest, most reliable part, step or rule is the one that is gone. Reviews reward deletion (S049); deleting a line of code earns two points, adding one earns one (S066).
- **Parts:** Is it needed to solve the problem now (the Starship door, S009)? Merge pieces (171 to two, S054); remove a whole layer (S055); drop content the user does not need (S056, S062); count robots and code as cost (S066, S068); settle a disputed part with a direct test (S069); delete legacy code slowly (S019); keep deletions reversible.
- **How to score:** High if the plan lists what was removed, the test behind each removal, and what came back. Low if every item survives on an in-case argument.
- **Quote:** "We're gonna focus on getting to orbit. We don't need a door." (S009) and "process is no process it weighs nothing costs nothing can't go wrong" (S049)
- **Sources:** 30 (S006, S009, S011, S012, S014, S015, S017, S019, S028, S030, S032, S034, S038, S044, S045, S049, S050, S054, S055, S056, S060, S063, S066, S068, S069, S083, S086, S091, S092, S094)

## 5. Build it yourself when the supply chain is too slow *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Vertical integration chosen by pace, not ideology. Use a great supplier if one exists (S080); if suppliers are too slow or there is no supply chain, build the input.
- **Parts:** Ask whether suppliers' best case covers you three or four years out (S039, S096); make the one part everyone waits for (S095); keep two sources and hedge only for a real reason (S056, S058); own an input to guarantee the customer's price (S092); do the least possible outside your core (S047); guarantee to buy a supplier's output so they build faster (S002).
- **How to score:** High if every key input has a plan B and capacity covers three years. Low if growth hangs on a vendor's roadmap, and also low if the founder builds what a good supplier sells (guidance from S047, S080).
- **Quote:** "not necessarily because we think that there's some religious reason to be a product integrator" (S017) and "we build them out of desperation" (S039)
- **Sources:** 30 (S001, S002, S005, S015, S016, S017, S027, S028, S032, S033, S034, S035, S039, S043, S044, S047, S052, S055, S056, S058, S059, S063, S065, S070, S080, S084, S092, S093, S095, S096)

## 6. One metric for the whole company *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Choose the denominator before arguing: one physical or unit-economic number, hard to fake, measured often and plotted.
- **Parts:** His picks: cost per ton to orbit (S014, S038); years by which Tesla sped up sustainable energy (S044, S076); miles between safety-critical interventions, plotted weekly (S046, S086); unregretted user minutes (S079); gross profit per employee with the supply chain counted, a friend's idea he adopts (S030); intelligence per gigabyte or per watt (S062, S095). State the top goal as an outcome (do not crash, S045, S066).
- **How to score:** High if the metric is hard to fake, moves with real value and is plotted weekly. Low if it is a vanity count (accounts, launches; for a clinic, leads instead of patients served, guidance).
- **Quote:** "the fundamental thing that needs to be fixed is the cost per ton to orbit" (S014) and "if we can accelerate sustainable energy by 10 years I would consider that to be a great success" (S076)
- **Sources:** 30 (S006, S007, S008, S010, S014, S017, S025, S028, S030, S031, S038, S039, S043, S044, S045, S046, S052, S053, S055, S060, S062, S066, S073, S076, S079, S083, S086, S087, S095, S096)

## 7. Physical-unit math: think in the limit, to the nearest order of magnitude *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Do the back-of-envelope in real units, push a variable to its extreme, get the answer right to a factor of ten before acting, and label guesses as guesses.
- **Parts:** Push to the limit (the whole car would need a 15,000-ton press, so cast a third, S068); count in units people feel (S014, S096); stock against flow (new cars are about 5% of the fleet, so a switch takes about 20 years, S076, S078); size from a target and publish the assumptions (S042, S047, S091); kill an objection with one physics check (S083, S097); flag each guess (S055, S085); refuse marketing units (S035).
- **How to score:** High if key numbers survive a factor-of-ten check and assumptions are written down. Low if a key number is a marketing figure, a straight line, or in no clear unit.
- **Quote:** "get the estimate to the nearest order of magnitude" (S033) and "If you take a particular thing and you scale it to a very large number or to a very small number, how do things change?" (S006)
- **Sources:** 29 (S001, S006, S008, S014, S019, S021, S024, S025, S033, S035, S042, S047, S049, S053, S055, S068, S070, S076, S078, S079, S080, S081, S083, S085, S087, S091, S094, S096, S097)

## 8. First principles (reason up from physics, not by analogy)
- **What it is:** Boil a problem down to the facts most likely to be true, reason up, cross-check against physics. Physics is the law; everything else is a recommendation (S072). He rejects copying others with small changes.
- **Parts:** Check conservation of energy (S006); compare options at real operating conditions, not catalog values (S049); copy the principle, not the form (S007, S046); when nothing sold works at any price, design the part from physics (S055, S061, S086); apply it to the factory itself (S041, S053).
- **How to score:** High if the plan names the physical or economic fact that limits it. Low if the only reason is that the industry does it this way.
- **Quote:** "boil things down to their fundamental truths" (S051), then "reason up from there, as opposed to reasoning by analogy" (S051)
- **Where it failed him (changed over time):** In 2016 Starship began with a carbon-fiber tank (S042); by 2019 steel was "the lightest possible reusable architecture" (S049). Newest (2026): "The point is, in retrospect, we should have started with steel in the beginning. It was dumb not to do steel." (S002)
- **Sources:** 29 (S001, S002, S003, S006, S007, S016, S019, S021, S028, S032, S034, S039, S041, S042, S046, S047, S049, S051, S053, S054, S055, S058, S061, S067, S072, S078, S086, S093, S096)

## 9. Close the loop on reality *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Build feedback that tells you fast when you are wrong: criticism over praise, outside graders over self-grading, real use over slides.
- **Parts:** Ask people close to you what they dislike, since they will not offer it (S003, S078); do not grade your own homework (S018); use the product in your own operation first (S046, S059); run new versions in shadow and on a fixed set of hard cases (S055); feed field failures back into design (S054, S056, S058); slides and canned demos prove nothing (S076, S084); one case is not a theory (S077); show customers the product working (S062, S086); treat every required user action as an error (S005, S008).
- **How to score:** High if bad news has a fast channel and the product is tested in real use. Low if feedback is praise, slides, or the team grading itself.
- **Quote:** "you're always to some degree wrong and your goal is to be less wrong" (S078) and "Tell me what you don't like." (S003)
- **Sources:** 20 (S001, S003, S005, S008, S018, S021, S032, S046, S054, S055, S056, S058, S059, S062, S074, S076, S077, S078, S084, S086)

## 10. Cost from raw materials, the million-units test, and the game of pennies
- **What it is:** Price a product from its materials; that is the floor, and the gap is how atoms are put into shape. Ask whether volume really explains the price, then grind the rest out item by item.
- **Parts:** Price the materials (packs about $600 per kWh against about $80 of materials, S003; rocket materials about 2% of cost, S071, S077); the million-units test (S006); set the cost target at volume before price (S016, S096); find the dominant cost parameter (S070); the game of pennies: 10,000 items at $5 make a $50,000 car, so cut every item, and deleting parts beats shaving them (S055, S060); the cost grind after launch is harder than reaching production (S055).
- **How to score:** Guidance: the bigger the ratio of cost to material floor, the more is process and the bigger the cut. Low if the plan cannot state its floor or cost per item. *Idiot index* is not in this corpus; do not quote it.
- **Quote:** "If it's too expensive at a million units a year, then volume is not the reason why your thing is expensive" (S006) and "to get the 10,000 items to be you know $3 instead of $5 that's kind of what it amounts to" (S055)
- **Sources:** 19 (S003, S006, S015, S016, S021, S033, S044, S051, S055, S056, S060, S061, S070, S071, S075, S077, S080, S086, S096)

## 11. The manufacturing S-curve and the least lucky part *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Output starts agonizingly slow, goes exponential, then flattens. A new product ramps only as fast as the worst of its 10,000-plus items, and the newer it is, the slower.
- **Parts:** Expect a slow, bumpy start (S036, S056); a new supply chain stretches the S (S064, S065); walk the limiting part as it moves (S034); forecast where the curve ends, not the weekly slope (S034, S062); a new factory takes about a year to ramp (S053); size lines in steps of ten (S061); adoption follows the same curve while people extrapolate a straight line (S071, S076).
- **How to score:** High if the forecast is a curve with a slow start, names its weakest parts, and holds cash for the slow part. Low if it is a straight line from day one.
- **Quote:** "things move as fast as the least lucky and least competent supplier" (S070) and "it's always a stretched-out S-curve, so you should expect that initial production of Cybertruck and semi will be very slow" (S064)
- **Sources:** 19 (S002, S010, S033, S034, S036, S039, S044, S053, S054, S056, S061, S062, S064, S065, S067, S070, S071, S076, S084)

## 12. Aggressive timelines (the 50th percentile deadline) and his known bias
- **What it is:** Set the most aggressive internal deadline you could hit about half the time, because schedules expand to fill the time given. A long schedule means the design is wrong.
- **Parts:** Aim at the 50th percentile (S002); after each change ask whether it made the work faster (S049); a confident number and a separate stretch number (S094, S095); say the plan number will probably be missed (S061); attach odds and conditions (S080, S086); separate wish from prediction (S073).
- **His record:** "I’m pathologically optimistic on schedule." (S001) Full reusability was "next year" in 2024 and 2025 (S011, S016); Mars cargo was a 2022 target in 2017 (S037), fifty-fifty for late 2026 in 2025 (S038).
- **Changed over time:** In 2019 he gave hard public dates (S005). Newer (2025 to 2026): targets are aspirations (S033, S083), a regulator's date is "that's not a time frame we control" (S036), and he plans 20 GW expecting about 15 (S094).
- **How to score:** Good if the internal date is aggressive, the outside promise is the confident number, and slippage is planned. Misfires when customers, lenders or staff plan around the internal date.
- **Quote:** "I generally actually try to aim for a deadline that I at least think is at the 50th percentile." (S002) and "if the schedules long it's wrong" (S049)
- **Sources:** 19 (S001, S002, S006, S007, S009, S011, S015, S027, S029, S033, S036, S049, S061, S073, S080, S083, S086, S094, S095)

## 13. Time goes to the limiting factor (skip-level reviews and the floor) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The leader's calendar follows the bottleneck: weekly reviews of the limiting item with the people doing the work, almost nothing on what goes well.
- **Parts:** Skip-level reviews with no advance preparation, plotting whether the work converges (S002); walk the floor, sleep on the line in a ramp (S029, S057); segment the day (S073); most time on engineering, little on press (S022, S077); fix what fails before improving what works (S068); drop or merge what distracts from the one thing that must be done (S090, S096).
- **How to score:** High if the founder's hours map to the current limiting factor. Low if they are spread evenly or go to meetings, press and admin.
- **Quote:** "I’m a big believer in skip-level meetings where instead of having the person that reports to me say things, it’s everyone that reports to them saying something in the technical review." (S002) and "fear is not the mind killer, context switching is." (S031)
- **Sources:** 18 (S002, S007, S008, S022, S023, S024, S027, S029, S030, S031, S039, S055, S057, S068, S073, S077, S090, S096)

## 14. Rapid iteration with a risk dial *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Set how much failure you accept by who gets hurt and whether the bet is the company. Iterate fast where nobody is at risk; be extremely conservative where people or survival are.
- **Parts:** Dragon allows no failures, Falcon some, Starship the opposite (S009); many cheap tests prove reliability faster than a few precious ones (S049); push test units to the edges of the envelope (S012, S073); keep radical design out of bet-the-company products (S066, S067); size a bold bet so the worst case is small and stoppable (S068); no single failure may stop a safety-critical product (S043, S092); the Shuttle punished failed changes, so known problems stayed (S009).
- **How to score:** High if tests are cheap where failure hurts nobody, cautious where customers carry risk (for a clinic, patients; guidance), and no test can sink the company. Low if caution is uniform or speed lands on customers.
- **Quote:** "Starship does not have anyone on board, so we can blow things up." (S009) and "if you make a change and it goes right, small reward." (S009)
- **Sources:** 17 (S008, S009, S011, S012, S014, S018, S035, S037, S043, S049, S066, S067, S068, S073, S076, S085, S092)

## 15. The algorithm (five steps) and requirements with a name
- **What it is:** His ordered method for any part, process or plan, taught in full in 2021 (S014) and in short in 2023 (S069). The order is the point.
- **Steps:** (1) Make the requirements less dumb, each owned by a named person, never a department. (2) Delete the part or process step. (3) Simplify or optimize, only now. (4) Accelerate cycle time. (5) Automate, last.
- **Requirements with a name:** Trace a part to its owner and test it (a beam the crash team said did nothing, S069); payments kept going after the approver left (S012); no payment code and reason, no payment (S073); a tenth of requirements can double a project's cost (S076).
- **How to score:** 5 if requirements and recurring costs have named owners, steps were deleted, and about 10% of deletions came back; 3 if it simplifies but deletes nothing; 1 if it automates a process nobody questioned.
- **Quote:** "make your requirements less dumb. Your requirements are definitely dumb." (S014) and "whatever requirement or constraint you have, it must come with a name, not a department." (S014)
- **His own misuse:** "now I have personally made the mistake of going backwards on all five steps multiple times" (S014), automating first and deleting last (S069).
- **Sources:** 17 (S001, S006, S009, S011, S012, S013, S014, S017, S019, S020, S034, S038, S049, S068, S069, S073, S076)

## 16. Is success in the set of possible outcomes? (impossible to merely late) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** First ask whether success is physically and economically possible. If it is, low odds are acceptable; break the "impossible" into parts until it is merely late. If success leaves the set, act drastically.
- **Parts:** Bracket the outcomes before committing years (S071); check that no new physics is needed (S080, S091); write down the odds (under 10% for SpaceX and Tesla, S022, S077); decompose an impossible timeline and take what exists now (Colossus, S002, S021); invent a new piece when the usual game is lost (the Model 3 tent line, S084).
- **How to score:** Go if success is in the set, the upside is worth it even at 10% odds, and the founder survives losing. On execution, high if each part of the impossible timeline has an owner and a workaround.
- **Quote:** "I’ll take drastic action only when I conclude that success is not in a set of possible outcomes." (S002) and "it wasn't possible now it's just late" (S085)
- **Sources:** 17 (S002, S003, S010, S015, S017, S021, S022, S028, S035, S038, S071, S072, S077, S080, S084, S085, S091)

## 17. Make it work, then make it efficient (crawl, walk, run) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Get one useful version by any means, then optimize, then scale. New technology takes about three major versions to be excellent.
- **Parts:** The fastest path to something useful, used in your own operation before customers get it (S046, S059, S093); a small version where mistakes are cheap (S002); conventional equipment first, modified at the choke points (S084); three major iterations (S015, S051, S055); prove the core, then replicate (S057); stay cheap and rearrangeable until you know what you build (S011); when the next version is far better, build a few of it, not thousands of the old (S085).
- **How to score:** High if version one is the smallest useful thing and versions two and three are budgeted. Low if version one must be optimized or full scale, or scales before it is useful.
- **Quote:** "In general, with any given technology, you first try to make it work, and then you make it efficient." (S001) and "it generally takes about three major versions in order to make it a compelling mass-market product" (S051)
- **Sources:** 16 (S001, S002, S011, S014, S015, S018, S033, S038, S046, S051, S055, S057, S059, S084, S085, S093)

## 18. Cash, the cash cycle, and control *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Hold cash to survive irrational times, get paid before you pay, and keep enough control to protect a long horizon, but not so much that you cannot be removed.
- **Parts:** Reserves and fast positive cash flow in a downturn (S017); shorten the time from raw material to customer payment, so growth makes cash (S044, S052); fit the plan to what you can afford to lose (S071); a little less capital efficiency is fine for much more speed (S065); equity before debt for a big move (S089), debt once cash flow is clear (S062); avoid a capital-heavy first company (S023).
- **Changed over time:** In August 2018 he planned to take Tesla private (S089), then 11 days later stayed public to focus on the Model 3 ramp (S090). In 2022 he called public control through one class of stock good (S046); newer (2025), about 25% so activists cannot oust him (S019, S026), but "not so much control that I can't be thrown out if I go crazy" (S062).
- **How to score:** High if runway survives a bad year, the cash cycle is short (for a clinic, paid at or before service; guidance), and control matches the horizon. Low if every month must go right or debt comes before cash flow.
- **Quote:** "make sure you're not running things too close to the edge from a capital standpoint" (S017) and "the faster you grow the more capital you have" (S052)
- **Sources:** 16 (S007, S008, S013, S017, S019, S023, S026, S027, S044, S046, S052, S062, S065, S071, S089, S090)

## 19. Fully considered: judge the whole system and the full cost *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** Judge an option where it is used, with every cost counted, never by one part's spec or the sticker price. Parts that are each right can be wrong together.
- **Parts:** Multiply every stage from source to use (S040, S051); judge at the pack, not the cell (S044); fully considered cost per mile or per use (S039, S060, S063); compare with the incumbent's real cost, not its subsidized price (S048); total cost of ownership shown as the customer's earnings (S052, S092); take on a cost you do not control if it inflates the customer's total (S056); use the worst case for your own side (S040, S092); pick a site on all costs, not the biggest incentive (S076).
- **How to score:** High if every cost to customer and company is counted at the level of use, worst case for your side. Low if it rests on one spec, a sticker price or a subsidy headline.
- **Quote:** "they got like 50 different answers and they were all true individually but they were not true collectively" (S066) and "These are real numbers and it only gets better than this. This is a worst case scenario." (S092)
- **Sources:** 15 (S025, S039, S040, S042, S044, S048, S051, S052, S056, S060, S062, S063, S066, S076, S092)

## 20. Iteration rate is the moat *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Progress is iterations times progress per iteration, so cut the time between them. The lasting edge is that speed plus talent, not patents.
- **Parts:** Keep the whole loop under one roof (S064, S065, S084); thousands of small changes between flights (S086); two sites competing (S049); going faster exposes hidden problems (S066); flat hierarchy, fast communication (S080); manufacturing is hardest to copy (S053, S056); the real rival is the incumbent product (S088); rivals copy your slides frame by frame (S062).
- **How to score:** High if the plan shortens the loop and counts iterations per month. Low if it relies on secrecy, patents or one big launch.
- **Quote:** "how many iterations per unit of time" (S084) and "Technology leadership is not defined by patents" (S088)
- **Sources:** 14 (S043, S045, S049, S053, S056, S062, S064, S065, S066, S080, S084, S086, S088, S094)

## 21. Staged rollout with proof gates *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Release to people one step at a time, widen only after a measured gate, and expect each extra nine of reliability to be ten times harder.
- **Parts:** Toe, toes, then a foot (S061); the safest group or simplest task first (S046, S053); one city at a time with a safety driver for about three months (S063, S064); gate the risky step on consecutive successes (S086, S091); assets you control before outside ones (S062); the bar is set by headlines, since one failure is world news (S061, S084); show safety with data, then ask regulators (S059).
- **How to score:** High if each widening step has a numeric gate and a stop rule. Low if it launches everywhere at once or the gate is a date, not a result.
- **Quote:** "put a few more toes in the water then put a foot in the water" (S061) and "each nine is an order of magnitude difficulty increase" (S045)
- **Sources:** 14 (S034, S039, S045, S046, S053, S059, S061, S062, S063, S064, S065, S084, S086, S091)

## 22. Everyone is chief engineer (the leader must know the product)
- **What it is:** People must see the whole system to tell when a local optimization hurts it, and the person in charge must understand the work well enough to judge who is good.
- **Parts:** Teams give right answers to the wrong question, and the org chart shows up in the product (S066); he is chief engineer while a COO runs operations (S012, S077); without understanding the work you cannot tell a good engineer (S086); use the company's products (S054); a CEO who never visits the factory is the warning sign (S015); teach the why before the tools (S079, S081).
- **How to score:** High if decision makers can explain how the product is made and delivered, and staff know why each step exists. Low if it is run by people who never touch it.
- **Quote:** "people need to understand the system at a high level to know when they are making a bad optimization" (S014) and "if you're the cavalry captain, you should know how to ride a horse." (S015)
- **Sources:** 13 (S006, S012, S014, S015, S021, S030, S037, S054, S066, S077, S079, S081, S086)

## 23. Hire for evidence of exceptional ability *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Ask for specific evidence of exceptional work, weigh the conversation over the resume, and hire traits first (talent, drive, trustworthiness, goodness of heart). Excellence beats headcount.
- **Parts:** Three bullet points that make you say wow (S002, S053); the story of their life and their hardest problems, since detail separates real solvers (S078); no magic from a big-company brand (S002); average hires can slow a program (S053); most responsibility to the most talented (S086); reorganize at each growth phase, because 5% wrong a year compounds (S058).
- **Changed over time:** In 2015 he blamed his hiring mistakes on weighing intellect alone, not effect on others (S080). Newest (2026): "And I think goodness of heart is important. I underweighted that at one point." (S002)
- **How to score:** High if the case is specific achievements tested in conversation, plus effect on the team. Low if it rests on credentials, brand or headcount.
- **Quote:** "Generally, the things I ask for are bullet points for evidence of exceptional ability." (S002)
- **Sources:** 12 (S002, S008, S017, S020, S023, S032, S053, S054, S058, S078, S080, S086)

## 24. Utility delta times people (what to work on) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Size a project by how much better it is than the current best, times how many people it reaches; then the output must be worth more than the input.
- **Parts:** Improvement times reach (S022, S046); how many years it speeds up what would happen anyway, with a victory condition (S028, S071); pick problems by effect on the future, not financial rank (S051, S077, S081); money follows usefulness (S020).
- **How to score:** High if the plan states its improvement, its reach, and output above input. Low if the main case is money or status.
- **Quote:** "how much help did you provide to each person on average" (S046) and "just be focused on having the output be worth more than the input." (S020)
- **Sources:** 11 (S020, S021, S022, S025, S028, S046, S051, S071, S077, S081, S082)

## 25. Design for the real use (dedicated beats hybrid) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Set the spec from how customers actually use the product, build one thing for one job, and drop what that use does not need. A retrofit of the old form is a horseless carriage (S063).
- **Parts:** Spec from the real distribution (most truck routes under 250 miles, S092; most trips carry one or two people, S064); fit the customer's natural cadence and hide the wait in a pause they already take (S051, S092); dedicated beats hybrid (S060, S063); redesign around the new technology, do not retrofit (S047); fix the small failure that costs the customer a day (S092).
- **How to score:** High if the spec cites data on real use and drops what customers do not use (for a clinic, the visit built around the patient's actual day; guidance). Low if it copies the old form or serves every case.
- **Quote:** "if you're trying to swat a fly just use a flyswatter or not a cruise missile" (S043) and "That's the natural cadence of a trip." (S051)
- **Sources:** 10 (S043, S047, S051, S056, S060, S062, S063, S064, S070, S092)

## 26. Garbage collection for rules *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** Rules pile up like code never removed, until nothing can move. Removal should be easier than addition, and rules should expire unless renewed.
- **Parts:** A sunset on every rule (S006, S070); 60% to pass, over 40% to remove (S070, S077); laws short enough to read (S077); rules are immortal, people are not (S075); put a rule back if people want it (S015); follow nearly all rules, object only where one fails its purpose (S072).
- **How to score:** He applies it mostly to government; for a company, it fits policy, approvals and sign-offs (inferred from S014, S015). High if the plan retires old rules. Low if each problem gets a new rule.
- **Quote:** "you have to have basically a garbage collection process for rules and regulations" (S081) and "any law should come with a sunset with a built-in sunset provision. If it's not good enough to be voted back in, maybe it shouldn't be there." (S070)
- **Sources:** 10 (S006, S015, S026, S028, S030, S070, S072, S075, S077, S081)

## 27. The master plan price ladder (start high, step down, each step funds the next)
- **What it is:** Enter where buyers pay a premium at low volume, use that money to build a cheaper, higher-volume product, then cheaper again. Fund the long goal from a business that pays the bills now.
- **Parts:** Sports car, then about half the price, then cheaper (S040, S051); start with what you can afford, in a small, mostly manual factory (S041, S097); cost falls through design iterations and scale, and both are needed (S070); fund the moonshot from a cash business (S042, S091, S097).
- **How to score:** High if each step is affordable now and its cash and learning fund the next. Low if it starts with the mass-market product before it can build it, or spends money it does not yet make.
- **Quote:** "enter at the high end of the market, where customers are prepared to pay a premium, and then drive down market as fast as possible to higher unit volume and lower prices with each successive model" (S040) and "So we've got to service the sort of Earth-based needs to launch satellites and that pays the bills." (S071)
- **Sources:** 9 (S017, S040, S041, S042, S051, S070, S071, S091, S097)

## 28. Affordability, not desire, sets demand *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Most buyers are limited by what they can pay, not by how much they want it, so cost and price are the demand lever. Before calling weak sales weak demand, check whether the limit is production or price.
- **Parts:** Demand lives where wanting and paying meet (S042, S047); small price changes moved demand a lot (S047), and $10 a month matters (S058); orders against production in one ratio (S056, S057); move price both ways with the order book (S056), but do not gouge early buyers (S057); make buying take under a minute (S058).
- **How to score:** High if the plan knows its price elasticity, its order-to-capacity ratio and its cost path to a lower price. Low if it blames demand without testing price, or raises price on a backlog it cannot serve.
- **Quote:** "The limiting factor is their ability to pay for a Tesla" (S047) and "this is very much a production constrained situation, not a demand constrained situation" (S057)
- **Sources:** 9 (S042, S044, S047, S056, S057, S058, S063, S080, S094)

## 29. Incentives explain behavior *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** To predict what a person or organization will do, look at what it is paid and punished for (S012). Asymmetric punishment kills change; a fee on cost maximizes cost.
- **Parts:** Regulators are punished for approving, not for blocking (S008); fund managers are judged quarterly (S013); milestone contracts with at least two bidders, ready to fire one (S076); make abuse cost more than it pays (an $8 fee makes 100,000 fake accounts cost $800,000 a month, S069); pay customers to bring customers and cut the reward as the network takes over (S071); price the externality (S077).
- **How to score:** High if the plan pays people for the behavior it needs and makes abuse unprofitable. Low if it relies on goodwill against incentives.
- **Quote:** "if you incent one thing, that thing will tend to happen more than the other thing" (S076) and "you're incenting the contractor to maximize the costs of the program cuz they get a percentage" (S076)
- **Sources:** 9 (S007, S008, S009, S012, S013, S069, S071, S076, S077)
