## Service-business founder questions

### Pricing
- **Stance:** Price follows cost and affordability, not desire. Buyers think in monthly payments, so cut the payment, track live demand without overreacting, and do not gouge in a shortage.
- "demand is very much a function of affordability, not desire." (S047, 2023-03-01). His edge case: the rich "look at value for money, but you do not consider affordability" (S047)
- "basically price really matters" (S056, 2023-01-25); "We have lots of people who want to buy our car, but simply cannot afford it" (S057, 2024-01-24)
- Payments: "the vast majority of people buy cars based on the monthly payment." (S032, 2023-05-16); "the demand is driven by the monthly payment. Can I afford monthly payment?" (S060, 2024-10-23)
- Cost target first: production cost near $20,000 at a million units, then "price will be as a function of demand." (S016, 2025-09-09)
- Live data: "you don't want to overreact to these things because sometimes you get like little dips or you know reasons that are hard to explain" (S029, 2023-05-17)
- Shortage: a big Cybertruck price rise "doesn't feel right to us to sort of gouge" (S057). As a GPU buyer he grants that a scarce seller charges what the market bears (S059, 2024-07-23).
- Sell total cost: "its actual cost of ownership on a monthly basis is much lower" (S055, 2024-06-13). New tech "doesn't instantly become uh cheap and affordable" (S097, 2021-10-04)
- **Changed:** 2016: "A lower cost vehicle than the Model 3 is unlikely to be necessary" (S041, 2016-07). Newer, 2020: troubled that no truly affordable car exists, he sets a $25,000 target (S044, 2020-09-22).
- **Read:** built for mass products with elastic, financed demand. For a clinic the payment lens and the no-gouging rule transfer; the volume curve does not (inferred from S047, S057, S060).
- Sources: S005, S008, S012, S016, S029, S032, S041, S044, S047, S055, S056, S057, S059, S060, S061, S097 (16).

### Discounting, price cuts, and referral rewards
- **Stance:** Price cuts work because demand is elastic, but cost cuts must pay for them. Run offers in short renewable blocks, and taper sign-up rewards once the network sells itself.
- "we found that even small changes in the price have a big effect on demand, very big." (S047, 2023-03-01). Tesla cut prices in early 2023 and raised some later (S029, S056).
- Cost cuts offset price cuts so "we can be free cash flow positive" (S058, 2024-04-23). Margin floor: "do I think we'll sacrifice margins? Uh, I don't think so" (S063, 2025-10-22)
- Rivals' discounting: "we don't see this as a long-term issue" (S059, 2024-07-23)
- PayPal paid people to join and refer, then cut the reward once "the value of the network itself exceeded any sort of carrot that we could offer." (S071, 2013-04-22)
- Tesla referral and FSD transfer offers are reviewed a quarter at a time (S055, 2024-06-13): "extend it for at least another quarter" (S033, 2025-11-06)
- Sources: S029, S033, S047, S055, S056, S058, S059, S063, S069, S071 (10).

### Cost per unit
- **Stance:** Price the product from its raw materials up. The gap between material cost and actual cost is a process problem, not a volume problem. Run the company on one fully considered cost metric.
- "What is the spot market value of the material constituents?" (S003, 2012-09-07)
- "If it's too expensive at a million units a year, then volume is not the reason why your thing is expensive" (S006, 2021-12-28); "what's actually causing things to be expensive is how you put the atoms into the desired shape." (S006)
- Anything made in volume tends toward its materials cost, he says, except where IP adds cost (S015, 2024-09-09). A rocket's raw materials are only 1 to 2% of its historical cost (S021, 2025-06-19).
- Change the material: "twenty five hundred dollars a ton for the steel" against $130,000 for carbon fiber (S049, 2019-09-29); "But it costs 50x less in raw material and is very easy to work with." (S002, 2026-02-05)
- One metric: Starship "is minimizing cost per ton to orbit, and ultimately cost per ton to the surface of Mars." (S006). Cybercab is built around fully considered cost per mile (S039, 2026-01-28); EVs around "the cost uh per unit of energy of the battery" (S070, 2016-06-02)
- His claimed results: Falcon 1 near $6 million against a class near $25 million (S071, 2013-04-22); Falcon 9 at "about half to a third the cost of alternatives" (S097, 2021-10-04)
- **Limit:** built for physical goods made in volume; it says little about services, where skilled labor is the cost (inferred from S003, S006, S015, S021).
- Sources: S002, S003, S006, S014, S015, S021, S025, S033, S039, S044, S048, S049, S050, S070, S071, S097 (16).

### Cutting costs and overhead
- **Stance:** Audit spend nobody owns, cut the obvious large losses instead of chasing zero, keep personal spend small, and spend big only on bets with a clear return.
- "there were more people making the food than eating the food." (S019, 2025-10-31), at Twitter.
- "and no one ever turned the money off." (S012, 2025-10-31), and "you actually can't manage to zero fraud." (S012)
- "We've cut that code from 700 000 lines to 70 000 lines" (S074, 2023-04-22), on Twitter.
- After long booms, cut duplication: "we've had a long long period of prosperity from 2019 to now" (S058, 2024-04-23)
- Big capex needs a return case: he calls a sharp capex rise justified by future revenue (S064, 2026-04-22); "when we do spend capital, it is spent very efficiently" (S039, 2026-01-28)
- Personal signal: "I only have one uh part-time assistant" (S031, 2023-05-23); "I don't actually own any vacation homes." (S096, 2025-11-20)
- Sources: S012, S019, S031, S039, S055, S058, S064, S065, S069, S074, S096 (11).

### Hiring for talent versus credentials
- **Stance:** Hire for evidence of exceptional ability, drive, and trustworthiness. Believe the interaction over the resume. Degrees and MBAs are optional, and character counts more than he once thought.
- "Generally, the things I ask for are bullet points for evidence of exceptional ability." (S002, 2026-02-05); "don't look at the resume. Just believe your interaction." (S002). Same in 2021: "not whether somebody graduated from a particular school or whatever" (S053, 2021-10-07)
- "Generally, I think it's a good idea to hire for talent and drive and trustworthiness." (S002); "Most engineering is done by people who don't have PhDs." (S002)
- MBAs: "people look at mba school as like i want to parachute into being the boss instead of earning it" (S066, 2021-02-02); "they can present well but they don't actually know how things work" (S066)
- **Correction, two dates:** 2015: his mistake was "looking too much at their intellectual capability alone and not on how they affect those around them" (S080, 2015-07-02). 2026: "And I think goodness of heart is important. I underweighted that at one point." (S002)
- Graduates deliver: Optimus credit goes to "new engineers many of them that are just out of college" (S063, 2025-10-22)
- Recruit with the work: AI Day targeted "anyone who is interested in solving real-world ai problems" (S045, 2021-08-19). Talent ranks first: "are you able to attract the best talent?" (S096, 2025-11-20). AGI engineers he courted "refused" Tesla (S059, 2024-07-23).
- "I honestly think people should move from like law and finance into manufacturing" (S061, 2025-01-29); "don't waste your time going into grad school. Start a company" (S035, 2026-01-06)
- Sources: S001, S002, S017, S020, S022, S023, S032, S035, S044, S045, S046, S053, S056, S059, S061, S063, S066, S080, S087, S096 (20).

### First hire and the role you cannot fill
- **Stance:** Hire someone better than you where you failed. If you cannot hire the key role, do it yourself until you can, and know what that gap costs.
- "it's very hard to do design of a sedan i tried i failed" (S008, 2021), so he hired Franz von Holzhausen.
- "I ended up being chief engineer of the rocket, uh, not because I wanted to, but because I couldn't hire anyone who was good." (S021, 2025-06-19); "it's because I couldn't hire anyone." (S037, 2017-09-29)
- The cost: "So if I could have found somebody better, then we would have maybe had less than three failures" (S077, 2018-03-11)
- Zip2's hired CEO: "the company succeeded in spite of that person not because of" (S003, 2012-09-07)
- "you actively recruit the best talent" (S017, 2022-05-16); "we pay way above average" (S020, 2025-11-30)
- No recorded position on employee number one at a very small company.
- Sources: S001, S003, S008, S017, S020, S021, S037, S077 (8).

### Firing and headcount
- **Stance:** Judge by results, cut fast in a crisis and accept some wrong cuts, trim duplication after booms, and expect the top team to turn over as the company grows.
- "If somebody gets things done, I love them, and if they don't, I hate them. So it's pretty straightforward." (S002, 2026-02-05)
- Twitter: "desperate times call for desperate measures" (S029, 2023-05-17); "some of the people who were let go probably shouldn't have been let go" (S029)
- "we just had a situation at Twitter where it was absolutely overstaffed" (S074, 2023-04-22)
- Tesla's 2024 cuts went across the board to remove duplication (S058, 2024-04-23). Dead weight is never zero, his firms included: "okay it's not zero" (S069, 2023-01-04)
- "It's just not the same team. It's not always the same team." (S002)
- AI and headcount, 2026: "We would increase our headcount, to be clear." (S002); "we actually expect to increase headcount over time." (S039, 2026-01-28)
- Sources: S002, S029, S039, S058, S069, S074 (6).

### Retention, pay, and stock for staff
- **Stance:** Give everyone stock, promote from the floor, pay above average, and accept that wealthy or comfortable people may stop working.
- "We give everyone stock options." (S072, 2023-11-30); "There are many people at Tesla who have come and gone from working on the line to being in Senior Management." (S072)
- The hard part is how to retain "Great people to do the hard work of building cars" (S072)
- Those who get rich and stop: "that's totally you know understandable and no judgment" (S069, 2023-01-04)
- "Tesla is not a honey trap. We're demanding" (S046, 2022-09-30); he warns against jobs where "the output you actually have to produce is so low" (S046)
- "I disagree with the idea of unions" (S072), which he says split staff into us and them.
- Sources: S008, S020, S046, S069, S072 (5).

### Metrics

- Users: "you care about like number of unique real people that are on the system" (S017, 2022-05-16); "true user seconds of screen time." (S031, 2023-05-23)
- Trend: "cumulative output is a very clean exponential" (S054, 2022-08-04); "sometimes people fret a little bit too much about this quarter or that quarter" (S054)
- Progress gates: miles between interventions decides when FSD goes unsupervised (S059, 2024-07-23; S060, 2024-10-23); "are we making that number smaller" (S055, 2024-06-13), on deaths per mile.

- "if vehicle end of line is not moving that means there's something Upstream that's wrong" (S029, 2023-05-17); "the two nanometer is just a marketing number." (S035, 2026-01-06)

### Capital and raising money
- **Stance:** Solve for whichever capital removes the speed constraint, raise before the window shuts, fund the moonshot from a cash business, and risk your own money only at odds you have priced.
- "I figured I was willing to spend half the money that I got from PayPal with no expectation of return" (S071, 2013-04-22); "the fastest way to turn a large fortune into a small one." (S051, 2013-03-19)
- 2015: "if something is capital intensive, um, it's really hard to get going" (S023, 2015-10-08)
- Raise before the crash: "we kind of saw it coming" (S017, 2022-05-16), on PayPal's March 2000 raise.
- 2026: "If capital is the limiting factor, then I'll solve for capital." (S002, 2026-02-05)
- Cash business pays for the mission: "Starlink internet is what's being used to pay for, um, humanity getting to Mars." (S038, 2025-05-29)
- "it should get more capital and if it's not it should get less" (S007, 2020-05-07). With strong cash flow "we could obviously buy back shares" (S058, 2024-04-23)
- Starting from $1,000 today, he says he would just raise money, and admits his network makes that unfair (S073, 2025-12-09).
- Sources: S002, S007, S013, S017, S023, S038, S042, S051, S058, S064, S065, S070, S071, S073, S077, S091 (16).

### Debt and cash reserves
- **Stance:** Keep reserves, reach positive cash flow, and do not load a company with debt; debt is for products with a proven cash stream. His 2008 survival came from stretching suppliers and a last-hour round, not planned leverage.
- "make sure you're not running things too close to the edge from a capital standpoint" (S017, 2022-05-16); in a recession, reach positive cash flow fast (S017).
- "we need to bring in more money than we spend otherwise we're dead" (S044, 2020-09-22)
- **Debt, by date:** 2018: "I do not think it would be wise to burden Tesla with significantly increased debt." (S089, 2018-08-13). 2023: "really don't have any debt um and we've got over 20 billion dollars of cash" (S056, 2023-01-25). Newer, 2025: debt fits once a product has a clear cash flow stream; until then the balance sheet pays (S062, 2025-07-23).
- 2008: "if we'd simply paid our suppliers on time we would have gone bankrupt immediately" (S017)
- He never sold in the bad years: "they skirted bankruptcy many times but i never tried to take money off the table" (S097, 2021-10-04)
- **Read:** he preaches reserves and lived at the edge; by 2025 he says of Tesla "We're not on the ragged edge of death. Not even close." (S034, 2025-04-22)
- Sources: S001, S002, S017, S029, S034, S044, S054, S056, S062, S089, S097 (11).

### Going public, going private, and selling
- **Stance:** Public markets give capital and wide ownership but shorten the feedback loop and add overhead. He flipped twice, each time after a capital need or investor feedback. He holds what he builds; no recorded position on when a founder should sell.
- **2018:** going private "could be good for our shareholders" (S089, 2018-08-13); 17 days later "the better path is for Tesla to remain public" (S090, 2018-08-24), since "There is also no proven path for most retail investors to own shares if we were private." (S090). Verbal funding support was not a closed deal (inferred from S089, S090).
- **2021 to 2025, stay private:** "The board overhead for a public company is much higher." (S027, 2022-04-17); "the parasitic load of being a public company has just grown over time." (S033, 2025-11-06)
- **Newer, 2026, SpaceX public:** "There's obviously a lot more capital available in the public markets than private." (S002, 2026-02-05); long bets "will depress our short-term earnings" (S013, 2026-07-29)
- Costs: "the most interesting questions you'd want to ask me are the questions I can't really answer." (S085, 2025-07-26). Short selling: "i think it's immoral myself" (S066, 2021-02-02)
- Mergers: "I do think there's a lot of merits to combining the companies" (S095, 2026-10-21), but the vote is "quite a high hurdle" (S095).
- "I just try to build things. And then there happens to be stock of the company that I built" (S020, 2025-11-30)
- Sources: S002, S008, S013, S020, S026, S027, S033, S054, S065, S066, S067, S076, S085, S089, S090, S095, S097 (17).

### Focus versus diversification
- **Stance:** He advises against running more than one company while running several. He copes by giving each day mostly to one company, sending his time only to the current constraint, cutting variants, and refusing new lines.
- "I wouldn't recommend running two companies." (S023, 2015-10-08); "I sort of have a plate full running Tesla and SpaceX" (S070, 2016-06-02); "I got a lot of fish to fry" (S072, 2023-11-30), on phones.
- "fear is not the mind killer, context switching is." (S031, 2023-05-23); "predominantly one company on one day. So, today is a Tesla day" (S031)
- "There's no point in spending your time on things that are going right." (S071, 2013-04-22), repeated in 2026 (S002).
- Fewer variants: "They're just variants for the sake of variants." (S047, 2023-03-01)
- Small slices for bets: Starship had "less than 5% of the company" (S049, 2019-09-29); "Like 99% of what we do should be focused on Earth." (S085, 2025-07-26)
- The main thing: "by far the biggest differentiator for Tesla is autonomy" (S059, 2024-07-23)
- "you can think of Tesla as like I don't know a dozen startups in one company" (S063, 2025-10-22)
- "I have a habit of biting off more than I can chew and then just sitting there with, like, chipmunk cheeks." (S017, 2022-05-16)
- **Read:** his advice and his life disagree; take the advice, not the example (inferred from S003, S017, S023, S031, S073).

### Side projects
- **Stance:** Weigh any side effort against the main mission. Run it cheap and small, publish it for others, or drop it. Cool projects are fine if you admit they do not move revenue.
- "Or should I be allocating that time to getting us to Mars? I should probably do the latter." (S007, 2020-05-07)
- "We bought some second-hand machinery. It's kind of puttering along, but it's making good progress" (S025, 2017-05-03), on Boring Company.
- He published Hyperloop and stayed out: "I'm not an investor in any of the companies" (S070, 2016-06-02)
- Roadster: "it's the cherry on the icing on the cake" (S060, 2024-10-23); "I don't think it moves the needle massively from a revenue standpoint" (S064, 2026-04-22)

### Timing
- **Stance:** Enter when a technology hits the steep part of its curve, raise before downturns, expand only after the first market works, and do not forecast macro or short-term prices.
- Technologies have their time, at "a steep point in the inflection curve" (S022, rec. 2016); in 1995 "the internet stuff was happening right then and there" (S077, 2018-03-11)
- "people tend to extrapolate in a straight line" (S071, 2013-04-22)
- Second market after the first: the Gulf made sense only once China worked (S078, 2017-02-15).
- Booms: it starts "raining money on fools basically" (S017, 2022-05-16). "making macroeconomic uh prognostications is uh a recipe for disaster" (S054, 2022-08-04)
- His own miss: on the Twitter offer, "my timing was terrible" (S074, 2023-04-22), just before ad spend fell.
- "what matters is not Innovation but the rate of innovation" (S055, 2024-06-13)
- "it's always difficult to predict technology with Precision especially over a 10-year time frame" (S079, 2023-03-03)
- Sources: S001, S017, S022, S032, S049, S050, S054, S055, S056, S071, S074, S077, S078, S079 (14).

### Competition and being copied
- **Stance:** Welcome rivals and copying, because execution is the moat. Incumbents lose by not iterating and by chasing 5 percent gains. He rates China the hardest rival and calls his own lead early. Patents mostly protect incumbents, but disclosure still has a cost.
- "I feel like friendly competition is good." (S006, 2021-12-28); "when our competitors advertise for electric vehicles every time they do that our sales go up" (S054, 2022-08-04)
- Incumbents: "the management team wants to do I don't know, 5%, maybe 10% better than last year." (S096, 2025-11-20). GM could have owned the market by iterating past the EV1 (S008).
- China: "they work the hardest and they work the smartest" (S056, 2023-01-25); "In the absence of breakthrough innovations in the US, China will utterly dominate." (S002, 2026-02-05)
- "there was a button I could press that would delete blue origin. I wouldn't press it." (S072, 2023-11-30)
- Patents: "receiving a patent really just meant that you bought a lottery ticket to a lawsuit" (S088, 2014-06-12). **Changed:** at Zip2 "I thought patents were a good thing" (S088); he still sees "roles for patents" (S069, 2023-01-04) in drugs.
- "we actually, you know, encourage our competitors to copy us." (S094, 2026-08-04). **Newer caution:** "we find our competitors do a frame-by-frame analysis whenever we release something and copy everything they possibly can" (S064, 2026-04-22)
- **Limit:** he calls his lead early: "I don't think you could see second place with a telescope" (S056); "I could be wrong, but it appears to be the case that Tesla is vastly ahead of everyone." (S005, 2019-04-12)
- Sources: S002, S005, S006, S008, S009, S011, S036, S039, S041, S043, S044, S045, S047, S054, S055, S056, S057, S058, S059, S060, S061, S062, S064, S067, S069, S070, S072, S080, S088, S093, S094, S096 (32).

### AI in the business
- **Stance:** AI-only companies will beat companies with people in the loop. Compete on talent, compute, and data. Digital jobs go first, physical ones follow through robots, and he expects medicine to gain. His own tools failed on his hardest work for years.
- "You need uh talent, talented people. Um you need um a lot of compute, expensive compute, and you need the access to data" (S031, 2023-05-23)
- **Changed:** 2023: "I don't see AI helping us make cars anytime soon." (S047, 2023-03-01). 2024: "so far the AI has been terrible at all of those questions" (S081, 2024-05-07), on SpaceX work. Newer, 2026: "Anything short of shaping atoms. AI can do half or more of those jobs right now." (S035, 2026-01-06); "Corporations that are purely AI and robotics will vastly outperform any corporations that have people in the loop." (S002, 2026-02-05)
- Software: AI beats "90% of professional software engineers at writing software" (S013, 2026-07-29). Agents act "as though it were a virtual human." (S095, 2026-10-21)
- Pace: "an AI two years ago feels like something that should be in a museum" (S094, 2026-08-04)
- Product lesson: chat AI "just didn't have a user interface that was um accessible to most people" (S079, 2023-03-03)
- Medicine, as forecast: "imagine a world where everyone has access to the best surgeons literally everyone." (S096, 2025-11-20)
- Hype check: "it is eye-watering to see some of these valuations." (S021, 2025-06-19)
- **Limit:** his own AI circuit design attempt did not work yet (S035); the medical claims are forecasts.
- Sources: S002, S012, S013, S015, S021, S031, S035, S045, S047, S053, S056, S062, S063, S079, S081, S082, S084, S085, S087, S093, S094, S095, S096 (23).

### AI risk and AI regulation
- **Oldest (2015 to 2018), alarm and a referee:** "with artificial intelligence we are summoning the demon" (S080, 2015-07-02); "AI is a fundamental risk to the existence of human civilization" (S076, rec. 2017); "AI is far more dangerous than nukes" (S077, 2018-03-11)

### Work hours
- **Stance:** Hard things need serious hours. He works seven days, asks others for a 40-hour floor, keeps about six hours of sleep, and says his pattern is not advice.

### Buying companies versus building
- **Stance:** Default to building from scratch. Buy only when the target is fixable, the price matches the true facts, and it buys years. An acquired idea is not a working process.
- "my default inclination is to start things from scratch" (S017, 2022-05-16); "acquiring it has to be fixable." (S017)
- Twitter "would perhaps accelerate progress versus creating something from scratch by three to five years" (S079, 2023-03-03)
- "can't pay the same price for something that is much worse than they claimed" (S017); later he "just revalued the company at less than half of the acquisition price" (S074, 2023-04-22)
- Maxwell's dry electrode still took years to scale after the purchase (S044, 2020-09-22; S047).
- "We didn't acquire anyone. We just built it." (S034, 2025-04-22)
- Sources: S012, S017, S034, S044, S047, S054, S074, S079, S096 (9).
